In Southampton County, Virginia, a preacher named Nat Turner waited for a sign.

In Missouri, a farmer named Robert Newsom bought a girl named Celia. Her lawyers argued that the law protected "any woman."

John C. Calhoun called slavery "a positive good." Thomas Jefferson Randolph proposed gradual emancipation.

The Cherokee, Creek, Choctaw, Chickasaw and Seminole nations were forced off their homelands.

Traders marched people south in long lines called coffles, through Richmond and Alexandria, to New Orleans.

Below the planters were the yeomen. Historians call this paternalism. Some met in hush harbors.

Harriet Jacobs hid in Edenton, North Carolina. Frederick Douglass learned in Baltimore. Judge Thomas Ruffin ruled in 1829.

On January 1st, 1808, the ban took effect. By 1830, the census counted more than 2 million enslaved people. The vote failed, 73 to 58.
