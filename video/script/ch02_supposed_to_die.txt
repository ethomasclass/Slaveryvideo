To see how we got there, rewind about forty years.

It's 1790, and the first United States census counts about 700,000 enslaved people. Most of them live in Virginia, Maryland and the Carolinas, growing tobacco and rice.

And tobacco is in trouble. It's brutal on the soil. After a few seasons, a field is worn out. A lot of Virginia planters are switching to wheat, which needs far fewer workers.

So plenty of people at the time thought slavery might slowly fade away on its own. Leaders like Thomas Jefferson and James Madison wrote that slavery was wrong. Both of them also kept enslaving hundreds of people for the rest of their lives.

The Constitution even set a date. Starting in 1808, Congress was allowed to ban bringing enslaved people in from overseas. And on January 1st, 1808, it did. Cut off the supply, the thinking went, and slavery would slowly starve.

Hold that thought. Because that is not what happened.
